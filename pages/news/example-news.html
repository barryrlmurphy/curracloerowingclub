Three medals in Limerick — and a J16 four that lit the place up.

Curracloe sent thirty-one rowers to the Irish Indoor Rowing Championships at the University of Limerick. They came home with gold, silver, bronze — and a new generation of juniors that nobody saw coming.

CW
Coach Walsh
23 February 2026 · 5 min read

We left the boathouse at 04:30 on Saturday in a borrowed minibus and three cars. Thirty-one rowers, four coaches, two parents in the boot of the minibus making sandwiches at half-six. By eight we were warming up in the UL Sport Arena. By six in the evening we had three medals on the table and one J16 four that wouldn't stop hugging each other.

The Irish Indoor Rowing Championships are loud. Forty ergs in two rows, scoreboards along the wall, families on the balcony. For a lot of our juniors it was the first time they'd raced anyone outside the club. They held their nerve. Every one of them.

The podium

Aoife Ní Bhriain took gold in W Masters D 1×, finishing a clear five seconds ahead of the field with a 1:48.3 average split. She'd been carrying a niggle in her shoulder all winter; you couldn't tell. Watching her hold form into the last 250 metres was the moment the rest of the club found their voices.

Jamie Kelly took silver in J18 1× with a 1:32.7 split — a personal best by nearly a full second, and a Curracloe junior record. He'd been training through Christmas on the home erg his parents bought him. It showed.

And then the J16 four — Niamh, Saoirse, Conn and Eoin. Their first national appearance. They pulled a 1:41.0 average across the bracket and took bronze. The four of them have been training together for fourteen months. They've all known each other since primary school.

"It's not the medal, it's the people who pulled with you to get there." Coach Walsh, post-race

What we did differently this year

Three changes from last winter. The first was the new 2k test protocol — every member did a full benchmark on the first Saturday of every month, October to February. We had the data to put the right people in the right brackets. No surprises on race day.

The second was video review. We mounted a phone on a tripod at the catch and at the finish and watched the playback as a group on Tuesdays. People made adjustments they couldn't feel by themselves. Stroke rate dropped, splits dropped with it.

The third was the two new Concept2 RowErgs in the shed, funded by Kinetic Financial Advice in October. Seven ergs total means we can run two Limerick-style 2k tests side by side. No queueing. The booking sheet on the wall finally has gaps in it.

Thank you

To the parents who drove. To Maeve and Daithí and Sinéad, who coached through the worst of January. To Kinetic Financial Advice, who keep the lights on and the heaters running in the shed. To every member who rowed the support pieces beside the racers in training — you're the reason they got to the start line.

What's next

Two weeks off the water for the racers. Then back to it: the flat water season opens 14 March, and the 24-Hour Rowathon is on the calendar for the second weekend in February next year. Talk to Maeve if you want to get involved with either.
